# Registry

`registry.json` is generated by `plan/registry.py` (`make -C emulation registry`) from
`plan/controls.yaml`, the databases in `EPICS/Db`, both `st.cmd` files and, for the simulators,
`ports.sim.cmd`, `emulation/compose.yaml` and `emulation/sim/config/*.yaml`. Do not edit it. It is
the only place where the GUI and the tests learn device and PV names.

## Top level

- `iocs`: per IOC its `roles` (`heartbeat`, `mode`) and `pvs`.
- `devices`: one entry per device that has EPICS records. Hardware without records (lens 3) and
  planned devices are not listed.
- `simulators`: each name mapped to `type`, `host`, `port`, `control_port`, `config`, `ioc` and
  `asyn_port`.

## A device

- `name`, `model`, `class` (widget class), `subsystem`, `page`, `ioc`, `status`.
- `flags`, each with a reason: `assumed` (the device rests on an assumption) and `not_installed` (records kept, hardware not there; the GUI hides it). `not_polled` is still known but no device uses it since lens 3 lost its records.
- `roles`: role to PV name. This includes the identity roles `id`, `firmware` and `channel_list`
  of libcom1 devices. Supplies: `vset`, `vread`, `power`, `out_cmd`, `out_state`, plus:
  - TDK: `iset`, `iread`, `mode`, `fault`.
  - Matsusada AU and REK: `vget`, `ilimit` (the current LIMIT, `current:limit`; its alias
    `current:setpoint` is listed under that PV's `aliases`, not as a PV of its own),
    `ilimit_get` (the limit the supply reports), `iread`, and `status` (remote/local).
- `labels`: the label of a device-level role, for example `{"p": "nAIM, six-way cross"}`.
- `states`: words of a device-level role, index = value, for a record whose database has none.
  For example `{"open": ["closed", "open"]}` for `interlock_open`, where any non-zero value
  means open.
- `channels`: per channel, in this order:
  - `label`: null where the channel has no name.
  - `spare: true`: the channel is not connected (valves s5 to s8, sensor box flows f1 and f5).
    The key is absent on connected channels.
  - `flags`: `{"not_installed": reason}` for a channel known to be empty (the TIC's socket 3;
    plan: `channel_flags`). Treated as a device that is not installed: no tag, no alarm, no
    trend; the device window names it with the reason. Such a channel is listed under
    `not_installed_channels`, not under `channels`, and its PVs under `not_installed_pvs`.
  - `class`: the widget class of a channel that has one of its own (plan: `channel_classes`):
    the TIC station's pumps `turbo` and `backing` are shown with the widget of a turbopump.
    Absent elsewhere: a channel is shown by its device's widget.
  - `states`: words of the channel, index = value, for example `["closed", "open"]` for the
    valves s1 to s3 and `["out", "in"]` for the Faraday cup actuator s4. They apply to both the
    command and the read-back of the channel.
  - `roles`.
- `simulators`, `loads`, `pvs`.
- `not_installed_pvs`: the PVs of a channel flagged not installed, in the form of `pvs`, kept
  apart so that the system tests and the logger, which read `pvs`, leave them out.

## A PV

- `type`, `access` (`r`/`w`), `egu`, `desc`.
- `role` and `channel`, or `internal: true`.
- `aliases`: other PV names of the same record, if it has any.
- `states`: the state words from the database (bi/bo `ZNAM`, `ONAM`; mbbi/mbbo `ZRST` …), index =
  value. For example, `status:read` of the Matsusada supplies is
  `["OFF", "ON", "OFF, LOCAL", "ON, LOCAL"]`, and the last two are MINOR.
- A written PV also has:
  - `test`: safe range `[low, high]`.
  - `ack`: the device answers the set.
  - `readback`: a PV name, or null.
  - `note`: what a test or the GUI must know, where there is something.
- `confirm`: `{"value": 1, "text": "..."}` on a written PV, or a list of them (one per value).
  Ask the operator with this text before writing that value; other values are written without
  asking. A question may carry `"warn": {"pv": ..., "unless": 0, "text": "..."}`: the warning is
  shown in the question unless that PV is connected, valid and reads `unless`. Today the vent
  valve `VALVE:s3:set` (open), and the TIC cart's pumps (T32): start and stop of each, the
  turbopump's stop with a warning while the foreline valve `VALVE:s1:get` does not read closed.
- `sim`: the simulator behind a StreamDevice record, as `name` or `name:unit`.
- `fails_with`: the simulators whose **silence** turns the PV INVALID (line up, no answer; also
  garbled replies, except for the NT 20 `raw` record).
- `fails_closed`: the simulators whose **closed connection** turns the PV INVALID. It is a superset
  of `fails_with`: I/O Intr records of a listen-only port fail only on a closed connection.
- About `fails_with` and `fails_closed`:
  - Both are written as `name` or `name:unit` (a fault on the whole simulator covers all its
    units), and both are absent when empty.
  - They describe the running state after the first contact, and name only what a client can rely
    on. A record that nothing processes during the fault, or that fails only when written or
    switched, is not listed. The `ack` and the read-back of written PVs cover those.
  - The rule is in `plan/build.py` (`fault_analysis`); the per-record statements are under
    `databases: <db>: invalid_on` in `plan/controls.yaml`.
